- **A12** The hypothetical engine, core concept: a closed, reversible thermodynamic cycle that uses matter–antimatter annihilation to produce work, then reverses the process to regenerate fuel with minimal entropy increase.
- **A13** Engine steps 1–2, fuel and annihilation: electron–positron pairs stored separately (e⁻ in a magnetic bottle (Penning trap), e⁺ in a separate magnetic bottle with opposite field polarity), controlled mixing in the annihilation chamber; magnetic confinement ensures they meet at the center; e⁻ + e⁺ → 2γ (each 511 keV), photons emitted back-to-back in the center-of-mass frame.
- **A14** Engine step 3, photon capture and work extraction: a perfect spherical gamma-ray mirror (fictional material) reflects 511 keV photons without loss. Photons bounce between two movable mirrors (optical piston). Radiation pressure pushes mirrors apart → mechanical work. Mirrors connected to a generator or flywheel. Photons lose energy (redshift) as they do work.
- **A15** Engine step 4, photon recovery: after work extraction, photons are lower energy (E′γ < 511 keV). They are directed into a photon storage ring — a resonant cavity where they circulate.
- **A16** Engine step 5, reverse process: two redshifted photons from the storage ring are collided head-on. To overcome the momentum conservation constraint, use a strong external electromagnetic field (or a heavy nucleus nearby) to absorb recoil. γ + γ (or γ + field) → e⁻ + e⁺, on condition that total photon energy in the center of mass ≥ 2mₑc² = 1.022 MeV.
- **A17** Engine steps 6–7, sorting and repeat: newly created e⁻ and e⁺ are mixed. Use crossed electric and magnetic fields (mass spectrometer style) to separate them by charge. Return particles to their respective magnetic bottles. Cycle repeats with the same particles (or their energy-equivalents).
- **A18** Engine energy flow (ideal): E_initial = 2mₑc² = 1.022 MeV for one pair at rest. After annihilation two photons, each 511 keV. Work extracted W = 2 × (511 keV − E_final). For the reverse process we need 2E_final ≥ 1.022 MeV, so E_final ≥ 511 keV, which means W ≤ 0. Thus the ideal closed cycle gives zero net work unless you add external energy.
- **A19** The "catalytic energy boost" idea: instead of just e⁺e⁻, use higher-energy annihilation (e.g., proton–antiproton) → more photons → extract more work before hitting the reverse threshold. Example: p + p̄ annihilation → many pions and photons, some energy lost to neutrinos, but usable photon energy ~1–2 GeV. The reverse process to make e⁺e⁻ only needs 1.022 MeV, so you have energy headroom to extract work.
- **A20** Efficiency ceiling claimed for the model: maximum efficiency in open-loop (using antimatter as fuel once) η_max ≈ 1 − mₑc²/E_γ,initial; if E_γ,initial ≫ mₑc² (from high-energy annihilation), η → 1. Closed-loop with perfect reversibility: net efficiency → 0% unless external energy is added (because the reverse step needs at least as much energy as you extracted).
- **A21** Critical challenges claimed for the model: (A) no material reflects 511 keV photons efficiently; (B) annihilation photons have random direction in the lab frame if particles have momentum, and capturing all photons is impossible without prior knowledge (quantum measurement problem); (C) pair-production yield is low unless photons are high energy and perfectly aligned, requiring enormous photon densities; (D) each step (emission, scattering, detection) increases entropy, and perfect reversal would require Maxwell's Demon to sort particles, which itself consumes energy; (E) storing positrons without annihilation losses is hard (currently done in Penning traps but with limited density and time).
